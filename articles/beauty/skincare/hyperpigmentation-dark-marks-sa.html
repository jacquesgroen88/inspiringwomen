How to Treat Hyperpigmentation and Dark Marks on Melanin-Rich Skin (SA Guide)

Inspiring Women July 25, 2026

Dark marks, uneven tone, and stubborn patches are the number one skincare concern for South African women with medium to deep skin tones. The good news is that hyperpigmentation is treatable. The challenge is that melanin-rich skin needs a specific, gentle approach, because the wrong products can make the marks worse. Here is exactly how to fade dark marks safely and keep them from coming back.

What Causes Hyperpigmentation on Darker Skin?

Hyperpigmentation happens when your skin produces excess melanin in certain areas. On deeper skin tones this is especially common because our skin reacts strongly to inflammation. The main triggers are:

  • Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH): the dark mark left behind after a pimple, ingrown hair, insect bite, or scratch heals.
  • Sun exposure: UV drives melanin production and darkens existing marks, which is why protection is non-negotiable.
  • Melasma: larger patches often linked to hormones, pregnancy, or the contraceptive pill.
  • Harsh products: aggressive scrubs, strong acids, and skin-lightening creams can cause more damage than they fix.

The Ingredients That Actually Fade Dark Marks

You do not need dozens of products. Focus on proven, skin-safe ingredients you can find at Clicks and Dis-Chem:

  • Niacinamide: gentle, well tolerated, and proven to reduce dark marks. A great starting point.
  • Vitamin C: brightens and evens tone while protecting against further damage.
  • Alpha arbutin: a gentle brightening ingredient that suits sensitive melanin-rich skin.
  • Azelaic acid: excellent for marks left by acne, and safe during pregnancy.
  • Retinol: speeds up cell turnover so marks fade faster. Start slowly, as explained in our beginner retinol guide.

The Non-Negotiable Rule: Sunscreen

You cannot fade dark marks while your skin is exposed to the sun. UV undoes every bit of progress. Wear a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every single day, even when it is overcast and even indoors near windows. This is the difference between marks that fade and marks that linger for years.

A Safe Routine for Fading Dark Marks

Morning: gentle cleanser, vitamin C or niacinamide serum, moisturiser, then sunscreen.

Evening: gentle cleanser, your treatment (azelaic acid, alpha arbutin, or a low-strength retinol a few nights a week), then moisturiser.

Introduce one active at a time and give your skin two weeks to adjust before adding another. If you notice redness or peeling, scale back.

What to Avoid

  • Skin-lightening or bleaching creams that contain hydroquinone at high strengths or unlisted ingredients. These are widely sold informally in South Africa and can cause permanent damage and a bluish-grey discolouration called ochronosis.
  • Harsh physical scrubs that create micro-tears and worsen inflammation.
  • Picking at pimples, which is the fastest way to create a new dark mark.

When to See a Dermatologist

If your pigmentation is widespread, has not improved after three to four months of consistent care, or you suspect melasma, book a dermatologist. Professional options such as prescription formulas and chemical peels can help, but they must be done by someone experienced with darker skin tones to avoid causing more pigmentation.

How to Prevent New Dark Marks From Forming

Fading existing marks is only half the job. If you do not stop new ones forming, you will feel like you are running in place. Prevention comes down to a few habits:

  • Wear sunscreen every day, because sun exposure is the biggest driver of both new and darkening marks.
  • Never pick or squeeze pimples, as this pushes inflammation deeper and almost guarantees a dark mark.
  • Treat breakouts early with a gentle spot treatment so pimples heal faster and leave less behind.
  • Be careful with hair removal. Ingrown hairs from shaving and waxing are a common cause of dark marks on the body, so exfoliate gently and moisturise the area.
  • Avoid harsh, irritating products, since inflammation of any kind can trigger fresh pigmentation on melanin-rich skin.

Consistency with these habits, alongside your treatment routine, is what finally breaks the cycle of marks fading only to be replaced by new ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to fade dark marks on black skin?

Most post-inflammatory dark marks fade within three to six months of consistent treatment and daily sunscreen. Deeper or older marks can take longer. The single biggest factor in how fast they fade is sun protection, so daily SPF is essential.

Is hydroquinone safe for dark skin?

Hydroquinone can be effective but should only be used under the guidance of a dermatologist, for a limited period, and at a controlled strength. The unregulated skin-lightening creams sold informally are risky and can cause permanent discolouration. Safer everyday alternatives include niacinamide, azelaic acid, and alpha arbutin.

Can I use vitamin C and niacinamide together?

Yes. Despite an old myth, vitamin C and niacinamide work well together and both help fade dark marks. You can use a product that contains both, or apply vitamin C in the morning and niacinamide at night.

Why do my dark marks keep coming back?

Recurring marks usually mean one of two things: you are not wearing sunscreen daily, or you are still getting the breakouts and irritation that cause the marks in the first place. Treat the root cause, protect from the sun, and resist picking at your skin.
